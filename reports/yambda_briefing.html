Our data

Yambda is a log of real listening on Yandex Music. Everything is anonymised, so we know what was played, when, and how it got to the user, but very little about what the music is.

The one flag the project is built on

is_organic

1 = the user chose it. 0 = the recommender served it.

Behaviour supports the dataset card: 65% of the first listens of a (user, track) pair arrive through recommendation, and 77% of organic listens are repeats. Recommended listens are played longer on average (69.6% vs 60.2%) but liked less (16.9 vs 20.9 likes per 1,000).

Careful

"Organic" includes replays of recommended tracks

A track discovered through a recommendation and replayed later counts as organic the second time. So organic is not "pure taste". We must say this in the report and decide whether we count first discovery or every play.

What we can and cannot do

We can
  • Compare the two streams for the same users (organic vs recommended).
  • Measure diversity by artist (distinct artists, entropy) using artist IDs.
  • Measure diversity by sound (audio spread, k-means pseudo-genres) using embeddings.
  • Build sessions (30 min gap) and study what is played together: 2.45M sessions.
  • Find similar users or tracks by shared listening (MinHash/LSH) or by sound.
  • Follow behaviour over the 301 days: 4,334 users have enough organic listens in both the first and last third.
  • Use feedback signals: likes, dislikes, percent played, as a measure of acceptance.
  • Test scaling honestly, since we have two sizes of the same data.
We cannot
  • No genre labels. We can only build "pseudo-genres" by clustering sound. They are not "rock" or "jazz".
  • No names. Artists, albums and tracks are anonymous IDs. We cannot name a recommendation or check one by ear.
  • No impressions. We never see what was shown and ignored, so no click-through rate and no model of "why not this one".
  • No recommender details. We do not know which algorithm or what it optimises.
  • No user details. No age, country, device or context.
  • No calendar time. Timestamps are seconds since the start (5 s bins), so no weekday, season or time-of-day effects.
  • No causal claims. Users who accept recommendations differ from those who do not. We report associations.
  • No lyrics or text. Sound is the only content signal, and 3% of listens have no embedding.

Fairness rules used in every comparison

Real listens

Only plays of at least 50% of the track.

Distinct tracks

Count each track once, not every play.

Same users

Both streams come from the same people.

Equal sizes

Equal sample sizes and basket-size distributions.

Watch out

Not every user is usable. Only 6,628 of 9,238 users have at least 50 real listens in both streams. Effect sizes are modest (+17% artists, −6% audio spread, about 2 of 15 genres). The tiny p-values come from sample size, so always report effect sizes and intervals next to them.

Section 2

Research questions and method pairs

Each question has two methods that can answer it. We run both and compare the answers: when they agree, the finding is stronger; when they disagree, that is a result in itself. Status refers to the 50M sample.

RQ1CoreDone on 50M

Are recommended listens broader or narrower than organic ones?

Method A · Diversity statistics

Per user: distinct artists per 100 tracks, entropy of the artist distribution, mean audio spread.

vs

Method B · Pseudo-genre coverage

Own spherical k-means on embeddings (k=32): genres per user and share of tracks in genres the user never picks in the other stream.

What we compareDo the artist view and the sound view point the same way? So far they do not: artists broader (+11 per 100 tracks), sound narrower (13 vs 15 genres). The report question is whether this holds at 500M.
RQ2CoreDone on 50M

Do recommendations make users more alike?

Method A · MinHash / LSH on track sets

Users are similar if they listened to the same tracks (Jaccard). LSH finds likely pairs without comparing all pairs.

vs

Method B · Cosine on sound profiles

Users are similar if the average embedding of their tracks points the same way. Optional: nearest-neighbour "twin" per user.

What we compareSimilarity by shared tracks vs by shared sound, in each stream. 50M result is mixed: slightly lower mean similarity in the recommended stream, yet 75% of users have a closer twin there.
RQ3CoreItemsets done, link missing

Which tracks are played together, and do they sound alike?

Method A · Frequent itemsets (A-Priori / PCY)

Sessions are baskets. Find frequent pairs and their lift in each stream.

vs

Method B · Embedding neighbours / session coherence

Nearest neighbours in sound space and mean within-session similarity.

What we compareDo the top co-listened pairs lie in the same pseudo-genre more often than random pairs? Equal number of frequent pairs in both streams so far; typical lift higher in the recommended one; sessions more varied there (coherence 0.417 vs 0.477).
RQ4StretchNot started

Are the "pseudo-genres" real structure, or an artefact of k-means?

Method A · Spherical k-means

Already written and validated against scikit-learn (ARI about 0.7).

vs

Method B · Graph communities (Louvain)

Track or artist co-listening graph, community detection. Covers the graph week of the course.

What we compareAgreement between the two groupings (ARI, overlap) and whether RQ1 conclusions change when genres come from the graph instead of sound. This tests our central measurement.
RQ5StretchNot started

Does the effect change over time?

Method A · Windowed diversity

Repeat the RQ1 measures in first vs last third (or monthly windows) for users with enough data.

vs

Method B · Embedding drift

Distance between a user's average sound in early and late windows, per stream.

What we compareDo users move more in sound than in artists, and does the recommended stream move them further? 4,334 users qualify on 50M; more on 500M.
RQ6CoreValidated on 50M only

How much accuracy do we lose when we approximate to scale?

Method A · Exact baseline

Exact Jaccard in blocks; A-Priori / naive counting.

vs

Method B · Scalable approximation

MinHash/LSH banding; PCY and SON for itemsets.

What we compareRecall, error, wall time and peak memory at 50M and 500M. On 50M: LSH recall 0.87–0.90 checking 4% of pairs; PCY counts about 100x fewer candidates with identical results. Hypothesis: counting about linear, exact Jaccard about quadratic, LSH in between.
RQ7StretchNot startedOutside method

Are accepted recommendations close to a user's taste in sound or in behaviour?

Method A · Distance to taste in sound

Cosine distance of a recommended track to the user's organic average; predict "played ≥ 50%" with logistic regression.

vs

Method B · ALS collaborative factors

Matrix factorisation on user-track listens; same prediction task using behavioural closeness.

What we compareWhich closeness predicts acceptance better. Fills the "outside method" slot if the brief requires one. Drop first if time runs short.

Section 3

Build on 50M, then scale to 500M

The same code runs on both sizes. 50M is where we make it correct. 500M is where we show it scales.

The pipeline (one pattern for every method)

PartitionRead the parquet one block of 2,500–10,000 users at a time.
ComputePer user or per session: diversity, spread, baskets, signatures.
Save smallWrite additive per-partition results as Parquet.
MergeSum counts, union candidates, one exact counting pass (SON).
PlotNotebooks only read results and draw figures.

This works because every quantity we compute belongs to one user or one session. It is the MapReduce / SON idea from the course, so it also serves as the "scaling" content of the report.

Why the small dataset comes first

Speed

Fast mistakes

A 50M run takes minutes. We can break things and fix them many times before one 500M run costs hours.

Correctness

A test with a known answer

The 50M results already exist. If the 50M users are a subset of the 500M users, the 500M pipeline must reproduce them exactly for those users. This is our best test of the code.

Tuning

Settings chosen cheaply

LSH bands and rows, support thresholds, k for k-means, partition size: tune on 50M, then fix them for 500M.

Contracts

Agree the interfaces

Five people can only work in parallel if the input and output format of each step is fixed. 50M lets us settle that first.

Risk

Find the memory wall early

We have 15.2 GB RAM (about 5.5 GB free with other apps open). Measuring peak memory per partition on 50M tells us the safe partition size for 500M.

Report

A scaling story for free

Same method, two sizes, timings recorded: that gives the scaling curves and the "does the conclusion hold" check without extra work.

Listens are 10x larger. Exact all-pairs work grows with the square of the users, which is why the similarity matrix grows about 100x.

Phase order

What we run, in order

  • Phase 0 Download 500M (the lead's script), extract embeddings for its tracks, check the user subset.
  • Phase 1 Move heavy notebook code into one shared module and scripts.
  • Phase 2 Re-run each method partitioned.
  • Phase 3 Scaling experiments: 50M vs 500M, partition sizes, 1–4 workers; log time and peak memory.
  • Phase 4 Check conclusions at 500M with effect sizes and intervals.
Don't

Pitfalls to avoid

  • Do not tune settings on 500M. Every long run must be justified by a 50M test.
  • Do not keep logic in notebooks. Notebooks plot, modules compute.
  • Do not run several heavy jobs at once. One run under about 8 GB.
  • Do not report a 500M result without the matching 50M number next to it.
